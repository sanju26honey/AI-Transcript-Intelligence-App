Expert 6 – Mark van der Meer
Role: Hospital Operations & Procurement Manager
Market: Netherlands

00:00
Interviewer: Thanks for joining us today, Mark. How would you describe robotic surgery adoption in the Netherlands?

00:17
Dr. van der Meer: Adoption is high in academic medical centers and large teaching hospitals. The Dutch healthcare model emphasizes centralized specialized care, so robotic systems are heavily utilized where installed.

01:05
Interviewer: What are the primary barriers to purchasing new robotic systems?

01:11
Dr. van der Meer: Strict health technology assessments (HTA) and rigid hospital capital budget caps are major barriers. Demonstrating net clinical superiority over advanced laparoscopic techniques is required before capital allocation.

02:05
Interviewer: How heavily do hospital procurement teams weigh ROI and financial budgets?

02:11
Dr. van der Meer: ROI and cost-per-procedure metrics are critical. We evaluate total cost of ownership over a 7 to 10 year lifespan, including instrument costs, maintenance, and facility modifications required for the robotic suite.

03:00
Interviewer: How do surgeon training and clinical outcomes factor into your decisions?

03:06
Dr. van der Meer: Clinical outcomes are essential to satisfy insurance and hospital quality benchmarks. Surgeon training is integrated into purchasing contracts; vendors must guarantee simulator access and initial proctored cases.

04:00
Interviewer: What is your 3-to-5-year outlook for robotic surgery in the Dutch market?

04:06
Dr. van der Meer: We project moderate, steady growth of 10 to 12 percent annually. Market expansion will focus on multi-departmental share models rather than single-specialty dedicated systems.

05:00
Interviewer: How long does the procurement decision process typically take?

05:06
Dr. van der Meer: The evaluation and procurement lifecycle typically spans 6 to 12 months, driven by formal hospital board review and multi-stakeholder committee consensus.
