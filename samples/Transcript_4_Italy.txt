Expert 4 – Dr. Matteo Rossi
Role: Head of General & Gastrointestinal Surgery
Market: Italy

00:00
Interviewer: Thank you for speaking with us today. How would you describe current robotic surgery adoption in Italy?

00:16
Dr. Rossi: Adoption in Italy is expanding, but it remains heavily dependent on the regional healthcare system. Top-tier public research institutes and private clinics in northern regions like Lombardy have high adoption, whereas southern regions lag due to tighter local budgets.

01:08
Interviewer: What are the main barriers preventing broader adoption across Italian hospitals?

01:15
Dr. Rossi: The upfront capital cost is certainly the primary barrier, but public procurement bureaucratic procedures also create substantial delays. Furthermore, proving multi-specialty utilization is difficult for smaller regional facilities.

02:10
Interviewer: How crucial are hospital budgets and financial ROI during purchasing decisions?

02:16
Dr. Rossi: Finance committees focus heavily on ROI. Clinical benefits like reduced length of stay are recognized, but CFOs demand proof that procedure volumes will reach at least 150 to 200 cases annually to justify the initial expenditure and annual maintenance contracts.

03:02
Interviewer: How important is surgeon training in your assessment?

03:08
Dr. Rossi: Surgeon training is critical. A system that sits underutilized because only one surgeon is certified is a financial liability. We require structured proctorship and multi-departmental training protocols before moving forward with capital investments.

04:00
Interviewer: What adoption trends do you expect in Italy over the next three to five years?

04:06
Dr. Rossi: We anticipate steady growth of about 10 to 14 percent per year. As multi-port and single-port options diversify and pricing becomes more competitive, medium-sized regional hospitals will begin acquiring systems.

05:00
Interviewer: What is the typical decision-making timeline for purchasing a robotic system in Italy?

05:06
Dr. Rossi: In public hospitals, public tender requirements and regional approvals extend the decision timeline to between 12 and 18 months. Private facilities can complete the process much faster, often within 6 to 9 months.
