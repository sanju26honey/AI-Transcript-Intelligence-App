Expert 7 – Dr. Sofia Lindqvist
Role: Senior Robotic Surgery Specialist
Market: Sweden

00:00
Interviewer: Hello Dr. Lindqvist. How would you describe current robotic surgery adoption in Sweden and the Nordic region?

00:14
Dr. Lindqvist: Adoption is relatively advanced in Sweden's university hospital centers, particularly in urology and gynecology. However, smaller regional county hospitals face funding constraints.

01:00
Interviewer: What are the main barriers to broader adoption in Swedish hospitals?

01:06
Dr. Lindqvist: The high capital outlay and instrument replacement costs are the biggest barriers. Regionally funded healthcare systems must balance robotic purchases against other pressing healthcare priorities.

01:58
Interviewer: How important are financial ROI and hospital budgets?

02:04
Dr. Lindqvist: Budgets are very tight, but ROI is evaluated holistically. Reduced post-operative hospital stay, lower readmission rates, and faster return to work are calculated into the overall economic value proposition.

02:55
Interviewer: What is the impact of surgeon training on purchasing decisions?

02:59
Dr. Lindqvist: Surgeon training is vital. Swedish hospitals prioritize ergonomic benefits and ease of training for junior surgeons to build long-term surgical capacity across multiple sub-specialties.

03:48
Interviewer: What adoption trend do you anticipate over the next 3 to 5 years?

03:53
Dr. Lindqvist: We anticipate steady growth around 12 to 16 percent annually as newer robotic platforms enter the European market, lowering total operating costs.

04:45
Interviewer: What is the typical hospital purchasing decision timeline in Sweden?

04:50
Dr. Lindqvist: County council procurement processes usually take 10 to 14 months from clinical initiative to final contract signing.
